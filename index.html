Bookkeeping for Dentists and Dental Practices: NHS and Private Fees, Labs and Hygienists

A dental practice earns in two currencies: NHS contract payments and private fees. The books have to keep them apart, because the costs belong to both but the income never mixes.

NHS and private income

The NHS contract pays a monthly amount against Units of Dental Activity, adjusted at year end. Private work — hygiene, whitening, implants, orthodontics — is fee-per-item or plan income. Record the two streams separately: NHS receipts reconciled to the contract schedule, private fees to the appointment book, plan income spread over the months it covers.

Labs, materials and associates

Lab work — crowns, dentures, orthodontics — is billed per case by the laboratory and belongs to the treatment it served. Materials and sundries follow usage. Associate dentists are usually self-employed on a percentage of the work they produce, with lab bills shared by the same split: the associate statement each month is the document the accounts are built from. Hygienists and therapists are either payroll or sessional on the same pattern.

Equipment and compliance

Chairs, suction, X-ray and sterilisation kit are equipment with servicing attached. CQC registration, GDC registrations, indemnity and cross-infection supplies are the standing costs of staying open. The surgery diary is the source document: fees, plans and associate work all trace back to it.
